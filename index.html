OrientationThe game in one page

In short

Thronewake is a persistent browser strategy game inspired by Travian. You develop several villages, produce four resources, raise an army, join an alliance and compete over a shared map. A world — an “Awakening” — ends when an alliance takes an Ancient Monument to level 100.

Three ideas are enough to understand the structure of the game.

Everything starts with the village. A village has eighteen resource fields, twenty-two building plots, its own construction queue, its own research and its own troops. An account owns several villages, but almost everything is decided locally. Only Culture Points and alliance bonuses are shared.

Food is a capacity, not a resource like the others. It is used to build, but above all to feed the population and the troops. An army that exceeds net food production starves the village. This is the constraint that shapes all military development.

The alliance is not a cosmetic bonus. Reinforcements, diplomacy, trade, map objectives and the endgame all go through it. A solo account can develop; it cannot win.

OrientationWorld, speed and rules

In short

Each “Awakening” is an independent game with its own map, its own speed and its own rules. Speed shortens durations; some values are configured by the server and vary from one world to another.

World speed acts on durations — construction, training, movement, unit research — and on several quantities: merchant capacity, storage capacity, traps, undefended loot. A ×3 world is not a ×1 world with numbers three times larger: each quantity has its own factor.

Technical detail

Storage factor by world speed: ×1 on a ×1 world, then ×2 on ×2, ×3 and ×5 worlds, and ×4 on a ×10 world. The capacity shown in game already includes this factor; the building's base table does not.

Merchant capacity: it is multiplied directly by world speed, on top of the faction base.

What depends on the world rules and must therefore never be copied from one world to another: map size beginner protection duration Culture Point thresholds alliance member cap endgame schedule undefended loot from Wilder Sites. The interface of the world you are playing is always authoritative.

OrientationProtections and pacing

In short

Three separate systems: beginner protection at the start, the daily Safe Time, and vacation mode for long absences. They do not replace one another.

Beginner protection

It blocks normal hostile interactions with other players. Its duration is configured by the world and displayed in game world-dependent. A single extension exists in the client.

  • It ends when an attack or a raid you launched lands on another player — not when it is sent.
  • Direct resource transfers are disabled. Below 200 population, only offers at 1:1 or better can be accepted.
  • Wilder Sites can be attacked and raided, but their loyalty does not drop.

Safe Time

Daily protection window set in the options. A change takes effect after 24 hours, and the number of changes per world is limited (three, excluding the first activation and adjustments of less than 30 minutes).

  • You can launch attacks, raids and scouts even if the arrival falls during your own Safe Time — but the attacking village's protection is then suspended until the troops return, plus 30 minutes.
  • You can recall your reinforcements and send back foreign reinforcements during Safe Time.
  • It is impossible to attack a normal village of The Ancients or an unclaimed Wilder Site during its own Safe Time.
  • Attacking a Fortress or an Ancient Wonder is allowed, but removes the attacking village's protection until the next day.

Rested Campaign: during Safe Time, a reserve builds up. It funds up to +15 % loot against normal Ancient villages and unclaimed Wilder Sites.

Vacation mode

Can be activated for 1 to 15 days. At least 24 hours before a manual stop, and 12 hours between two activations. During the absence, lumber, stone and metal production as well as training are paused; on return, they are doubled for an equal duration. Trade routes may be skipped: prepare your stocks beforehand.

Late arrival: a player who joins a world more than 24 hours after it opens receives doubled lumber, stone, metal and training, for a duration equal to the delay accumulated beyond those 24 hours.

Getting startedThe first hours

In short

Always keep a useful action running, never let a store cap out, and never build anything “just to fill space”. Every expense must set up production, troops, research or the next expansion.

The first ten minutes

  1. Read the tasks. They give resources and Research Points, and act as a guiding thread.
  2. Identify the resource that is blocking you. Upgrading a field you do not need while another resource is capping out is lost tempo.
  3. Look at the map. Neighbors, Wilder Sites, interesting tiles: your surroundings decide part of your military and logistical needs.

The first hour

  • Upgrade the fields you need, and the Warehouse and the Granary just enough not to cap out while you are away.
  • Build only the prerequisites of the next useful thing.
  • Understand that research is local before spending your first points.
  • Compare cost, Upkeep, speed and role before producing a unit, rather than picking the biggest raw stat.

The first day

  • Watch your Culture Points and the next threshold, shown in the Residence or the Palace.
  • Identify several candidate tiles before training Settlers.
  • Start thinking in terms of specialization: production, food, troops, logistics.
  • Join an active alliance. It is the most underrated accelerator of the early game.

Getting startedMistakes that cost you dearly

  • Upgrading everything evenly. An efficient village is not symmetrical: you invest according to the next objective, not to balance out a table.
  • Producing troops without food. The training cost is only an advance; Upkeep is paid afterwards, every hour, indefinitely.
  • Waiting until the last moment to expand. Culture Points, local slot, Settlers and target tile must be prepared together.
  • Using a single unit as a substitute for scouting. It resolves as an ordinary combat — see the combat model — so at best it tells you whether one threshold was crossed, never what is inside. Reconnaissance is done with scouts.
  • Letting resources cap out. Any production beyond capacity is lost.

EconomyResources and food

In short

Lumber, stone and metal are spent. Food is spent and consumed continuously: it supports population and troop upkeep. A village does not need to be self-sufficient — an empire does.

Watch out — the four resources are named

Lumber · Stone · Metal · Food. Players arriving from other games in this family will expect wood, clay, iron and crop; the interface uses none of those words. The community notation 4-4-4-6 follows the same order — lumber, stone, metal, food.

net food = gross production − population − troop upkeep

Population comes from buildings and fields. Upkeep depends on the units: a unit with upkeep 1 consumes 1 food per hour. Five thousand units with upkeep 1 therefore cost 5 000 food per hour before modifiers.

A negative balance is not automatically a mistake: a military village can be deliberately fed by others. But if food really does run short, troops starve, and foreign reinforcements die before yours do.

Technical detail

The Town Hall → Resources screen breaks production down and separates several upkeep entries: stationed troops, troops in transit, reinforcements, trapped troops, wounded troops and troops on Wilder Sites. Before moving a large army, check which village is actually paying its upkeep.

Embermark: the Rider's Wells reduces cavalry upkeep according to its level. The upkeep printed on the unit card is therefore not the effective value while that building is active.

Storage and overflow

The Warehouse stores lumber, stone and metal; the Granary stores food. Producing beyond capacity without spending, shipping or converting the surplus wastes production.

Technical detail — the storage calculation chain 
real capacity = building table × world speed factor × (1 + Compaction)

The three scopes are shown in three different places and must not be confused: the catalogue table is without speed and without research; the building's “Show all levels” box includes speed but not research; the resource bar tooltip gives the real capacity, everything included.

Related research: Warehouse Compaction +10 / +20 / +30 % capacity · Supply Depot +5 / +10 / +15 % capacity protected from raiding · Surplus Exchange converts 10 / 20 / 30 % of the overflow into your lowest resource.

Technical detail — what is actually observed above 10

A field at level 10 in a capital does offer level 11: the upgrade button is active, with its cost and its production step (+840 → +1 176 food/h on a Grazing Field). So the capital cap is definitely above 10. The figure of 22 comes from the client's code, and a field has never been seen that high in play unconfirmed above 11 — treat it as the ceiling the code returns, not as a level anyone has reached.

The 13 of a non-capital city, by contrast, is confirmed.

Watch out

Lumberyard, Quarry, Foundry and Farm are the visual containers for these fields, not a second level system. Do not try to upgrade them separately.

Refining buildings

Sawmill, Brickyard, Forge and Butcher multiply the production of their resource. Their cap is not 20 but 5 + the rank of the Industrial Expansion research, so 8 at most. It is an easy cap to overlook, and it holds back many accounts without their ever noticing.

Demolition and cancellation

A building can be demolished level by level, not only razed outright. Demolition is immediate and irreversible.

Technical detail

The refund is an exact percentage of the catalogue cost of the removed levels, set by the Material Salvage research: 35 % at rank I, 40 % at rank II, 45 % at rank III. It is not capped by the space available in the warehouse.

A Grand Work that is being progressively funded can be cancelled for 80 % of the materials already stored in it, clipped by the available space. This figure is documented for Grand Work only; whether cancelling an ordinary queued upgrade refunds anything, and at what rate, is unconfirmed.

A building destroyed by catapult leaves Rubble: clearing it returns 15 / 20 / 25 % under the same research rank, and rebuilding up to the previous level gets −25 % time by default, raised to −35 / −50 / −65 % by Reconstruction Crews.

EconomyTrade and logistics

In short

The Marketplace moves resources to the village that needs them. Four uses: direct shipment, player-to-player offer, recurring trade route, and remote collection from one of your own villages.

Each Marketplace level gives one merchant: level 20 = 20 merchants. The interface separates the merchants that are available, travelling, and tied up in offers. It is almost always that number that blocks a shipment, not the building's level.

capacity per merchant = faction base

Faction base: Embermark 500 · Verdant 750 · Stormfang 1 000. Embermark's Trade Office progresses twice as fast as the other factions' (×9 instead of ×5 at level 20), which closes most but not all of the gap: at level 20 and with no other factor, Embermark reaches 4 500 per merchant against Verdant's 3 750 and Stormfang's 5 000. Embermark overtakes Verdant; Stormfang keeps the lead.

Player-to-player offers

  • Both sides use their own merchants.
  • The ratio must stay between 1:2 and 2:1, and each resource offered or requested must reach 50 units.
  • Resources placed in an offer remain raidable. If they are stolen, the offer is cancelled. The Market Safekeeping research protects 15 / 30 / 50 % of them.
  • No trading with The Ancients, nor between two alliances at war.

Direct shipments and anti-pushing

Shipments to another player are capped according to the diplomatic relationship and population, with daily and weekly limits displayed. Between accounts linked by the same IP address, only 1:1 offers are accepted.

Trade routes

A route schedules repeated deliveries. It can be skipped for a specific reason that the game displays: missing merchants or resources, insufficient capacity, support limit reached, target no longer eligible, war, beginner protection or vacation mode. Read the cause before blindly upgrading the Marketplace.

In short

An account with enough Culture Points but no local slot cannot found, and vice versa. Most expansion blockages come from this confusion.

EmpireCulture Points

In short

All your villages produce Culture Points. When a threshold is reached, a slot opens up for one new village or one city. Both consume the same slot: it is an exclusive choice.

What produces Culture Points

  • The buildings and fields of all your villages.
  • Celebrations, which credit immediately.
  • The Civic Legacy alliance bonus.
  • City status: +200 per day and +25 % on the passive production of the village concerned.

Celebrations

Festival Grounds require a Town Hall 10 and an Academy 10. Two formats: the Feast, whose reward is based on the village that launches it, and the Grand Ball, available from level 10, whose reward takes the whole empire into account. Several celebrations can be queued.

Technical detail — cadence, not stock

Culture Points are credited at launch; the celebration's duration is a cooldown before the next one in that village. Cadence is therefore a real constraint on its own: past a certain point, holding more resources does not buy more Culture Points, only a shorter wait does.

That does not mean resources are free. At the rates below, a village running Feasts back to back spends on the order of 45 000 resources a day on them, and a Grand Ball costs about 100 000 per run. Which of the two ceilings binds first — cadence or income — depends on the village, and neither can be assumed.

Two levers on cadence: the Festival Grounds level shortens the duration, down to half at level 20, and every village that has Festival Grounds can run one in parallel. The second lever is the one players under-use: five villages celebrating is five times the Culture Points, with no research and no threshold to cross.

Efficiency per point, read on a level 6 Festival Grounds: a Feast gives 250 points for about 81 resources per Culture Point, a Grand Ball 1 000 points for about 102 resources per point — roughly a quarter less efficient. These ratios do not depend on the building's level.

Duration follows a linear law, from full length at level 1 to exactly half at level 20:

duration = base × ( 1 − (level − 1) / 38 )

The two base durations come out round: 12 h for a Feast, 30 h for a Grand Ball. That gives the cadence at the levels that matter:

Thresholds

Culture Point thresholds are set by the server world-dependent. The Residence and the Palace show, in their Expansion tab: the village's production, the whole empire's production, the slots used, the current stock, the threshold for the next slot, what is left to produce and the estimated time. This is the control screen for expansion.

EmpireVillage, City and capital

In short

These are three distinct statuses. The city is an upgrade paid for with a Culture Point slot. The capital is a status the game tracks separately, and the Palace is how you move it. A village can be one, the other, or both — but that last combination is not documented.

The six buildings that go to 22 in a city: Town Hall, Warehouse, Granary, Barracks, Stable, Workshop.

Watch out — the capital does not raise a single building

The 22 cap on those six is tied to city status alone. Capital status raises fields, and nothing else. A capital that is not a city keeps every building at the normal village cap.

And capital status is not something you pick from a menu: the capital is whichever village holds the Palace. Before any Palace exists, your starting village holds the status by default — which is why a village with nothing but a Residence can already show capital field behaviour.

City or new village?

The conversion consumes a Culture Point slot but no expansion slot. It therefore competes directly with founding or conquering a village. The right question is not the resource cost, but what the next Culture Point slot is worth.

Technical detail — decision grid

The city wins when the village in question is not the capital and is hitting several caps at once — fields at 10, Warehouse and Granary at 20 — or when loyalty 200 and the twenty Guards answer a real threat.

The village wins when you have the space, the food and the time to develop it: a whole village brings eighteen fields, twenty-two plots, its own research, its own queue and its own production of Culture Points.

What is not an argument: the three plots. They can also be had through Expanded Foundations, paid in local Research Points rather than an account slot.

Capital

The capital is the village that has the Palace. At the start of a world you own one village, so it carries the status by default, with only a Residence. Build a Palace in a second village and the status follows the Palace: the new one becomes the capital, and the old one reverts to an ordinary village. There is no separate ceremony and no choice to confirm — the building is the status.

Buildings that become incompatible on either side are demolished automatically at that moment: the old capital's Stonemason's Lodge, the new one's Great Barracks or Great Stable. Check both villages before you build that Palace.

If a capital is destroyed and other villages remain, the status passes to the largest of them; with a single village, the capital cannot be destroyed.

Watch out — moving the capital demotes the old one

This is the expensive part, and it is easy to miss because nothing warns you. A capital's resource fields climb past the normal cap of 10. The moment the Palace goes up elsewhere, that village is an ordinary village again, and every field above 10 is over the cap it is now subject to. What happens to them — frozen, capped, reduced — is not documented unconfirmed.

The practical reading: decide where your capital will end up before you invest in fields above level 10, not after. Levelling the fields of a village you intend to demote later is the one capital mistake you cannot undo cheaply.

Conquest protection moves with the status too: the capital is the village that cannot be conquered, so moving the Palace also moves that shield.

City Guards

Up to twenty Guards, hired one at a time in the Watch Tower, each costing resources, 1 population and 1 upkeep. They are not part of the normal army.

Watch out

If the Watch Tower is destroyed, all twenty Guards are lost and must be hired again. That is what makes the ram an indirectly anti-garrison weapon.

ResearchHow research works

In short

Every village has its own tree of 63 technologies, split into 3 paths and 9 guilds, and its own Research Points. Nothing is shared between villages: what you unlock in one place has to be paid for again elsewhere.

Research Points are produced by the villagers: the bigger the village grows, the more it generates. Some tasks grant them too. A technology, or the whole tree, can be reset with 80 % of the points refunded, subject to the reset prerequisites. The reset panel displays a 72-hour cooldown. Its exact scope and whether individual and full resets share that timer remain unverified.

Each guild contains three Minor technologies, three Major ones and one Keystone. Minor and Major have three ranks; a Keystone has only one. Ranks unlock in order. Three paths × three guilds × seven technologies = the 63.

Technical detail — the shape of a prerequisite

Every technology states its requirements explicitly on its own card. The three tiers do not ask for the same things:

TierWhat it requires
Minorthe RP cost · free Minor capacity · a building or field condition. No upstream research on the inspected example; check the individual card.
Majorthe RP cost · free Major capacity · a building condition, often with alternatives ("A or B or C") · its paired Minor at rank II
Keystonethe RP cost · free Keystone capacity · a technology-specific building requirement (Frontier Command explicitly requires Expedition Camp 20) · "Unlock any 3 Major ranks in this branch"

The mapping supported by the inspected cards: the tree presents Minor–Major pairs, and the four named Major requirements checked so far require the corresponding Minor at rank II. That is why a guild can look half-open with plenty of points left: you are two Minor ranks away from the Major you actually want, not short of RP.

Worked example: Industrial Expansion asks for 1 400 RP, Major capacity, "Sawmill level 5 or Brickyard level 5 or Forge level 5 or Butcher level 5", and Focus Production II.

Watch out — the Keystone wording is ambiguous

A Keystone reads "Unlock any 3 Major ranks in this branch". Whether that means three ranks spread anywhere in the guild, or one rank in each of its three Majors, is not settled: a guild with its three Majors at one rank each satisfies both readings, and that is the state the wording has been seen in unconfirmed. Plan for the stricter reading and you are safe either way.

Technical detail — every unit fights

Leaders, Settlers and siege units take part in regular combat with their printed attack and count towards the engaged headcount. There is no exception.

Corollary: a unit with zero attack makes everyone's losses worse, since it inflates the headcount without adding anything to the strength.

Scouts need three separate things kept apart, because they are not the same statement:

  • The scouting mission is resolved by its own law, in which only scouts take part on either side. That is the case where scouts are outside regular combat — the mission is sent on its own.
  • The contribution to strength is nil on attack: every scout has a printed attack of 0, so it adds nothing to A. On defence it contributes its printed defence like any other unit.
  • The headcount always includes them. A scout sent along with an attack counts in N, which lowers M and raises everyone's loss fraction.

So a scout inside an assault is not exempt from combat: it is a body that costs the army something and returns no intelligence.

ArmiesSmithy and upgrades

In short

The Smithy upgrades a unit in one village at a time. The gain grows with the unit's upkeep: units that are expensive to feed gain the most.

Technical detail — upgrade law 
stat(n) = base + ( base + 300 × upkeep / 7 ) × ( 1,007 ^ n − 1 )

It applies to attack and to both defence values. The 300 × upkeep / 7 term explains why a high-upkeep unit benefits far more from upgrades than a cheap one.

The upgrade level is capped by the Smithy's level; an Advanced Smithy research allows you to go beyond 20 without raising the building past 20.

These levels also apply to siege damage: an upgraded catapult demolishes more.

CombatThe combat model

In short

In an attack, the loser is always annihilated; only the margin of victory decides the winner's losses. Attacking with too small a margin is the worst possible choice: doubling the force engaged can mean losing fewer units in absolute terms.

Technical detail — the formulas 
N = total number of units engaged, attacker + defender
    (every unit counts, including those with zero attack)

M = 1,5                          if N ≲ 1 000
    2 × ( 1,8592 − N ^ 0,015 )   if N > 1 000

A = Σ ( quantity × attack )
D = ( troop defence + flat defence ) × multipliers × morale

ATTACK      loser: 100 %
            winner: ( loser's strength / winner's strength ) ^ M

RAID        x = ( loser's strength / winner's strength ) ^ M
            winner loses x / (1 + x)   ·   loser loses 1 / (1 + x)
            nobody is annihilated

SCOUTING    A = attacking scouts × 35
            D = defending scouts × 20      (regular troops do not defend)
            attacker loses min( 100 % ; (D / A) ^ M )
            defender loses 0 % — always

The constants 1,8592 and 0,015 are fitted to measurements, not read from the code: they reproduce the displayed figure to a tenth of a percent across the whole tested range. Scouts have the same combat attributes in all four factions: attack 35, defence 20.

Defence against a mixed army: effective defence weights anti-infantry and anti-cavalry by the share of attack points each class contributes to the assaulting army, not by headcount.

Watch out — a lone unit is not doomed by default

No special rule destroys a unit sent on its own: the ordinary model applies. A single Sun Rider, with no upgrades and no bonuses, sent against an empty village, survives and brings back loot — its attack exceeds the village's passive defence. A lone unit can therefore serve as a cheap probe, but it tells you one thing only: whether its own attack cleared the defence it met. It is lost against any garrison whose defence exceeds its attack, which in practice means most defended villages. Serious reconnaissance remains the scouts' job.

Two direct tactical consequences. The exponent M decreases as armies grow, which increases the winner's loss fraction at an equal strength ratio: at an identical margin, splitting is slightly more economical than striking as one mass — provided every wave wins. And since units without attack count towards N, taking scouts or ornamental units along on an assault costs everyone extra losses.

CombatA village's defence

In short

A village's defence is not only its garrison. Three buildings add to it — Watch Tower, Residence or Palace, City Guards — and morale multiplies the whole. Against an empty village, these passive terms are the entire defence. The City Guards are hired in the Watch Tower, which is why losing that building costs you both multipliers at once.

Technical detail — composition 
D = ( troop defence + total flat defence )
    × Watch Tower multiplier
    × City Guards multiplier
    × morale

The multipliers compose by product, not by sum. The flat term is inside the morale. There is a base flat defence of 10 points, independent of any building.

The Residence and the Palace give the same flat defence, 2 × level², in all three playable factions. The Ancients have neither Watch Tower nor Residence: their villages have no passive defence beyond the base 10 points unconfirmed outside the simulator.

How to read these terms in practice. Flat defence weighs little against a real garrison — a few hundred points against several thousand — but on an undefended village it is the defence, and it is far from a marginal addition there. With the document's own values, an Embermark Watch Tower at level 20 contributes 10 + 10 × 20 = 210 flat points against a base of 10: the fixed term is multiplied by 21 before any multiplier applies. Add a Residence or Palace at level 20 and the flat term reaches 1 010.

The two multipliers are not interchangeable either, and the Watch Tower is usually the larger of the two. At level 20 it gives 1,03 ^ 20 ≈ 1,81 for Embermark, 1,025 ^ 20 ≈ 1,64 for Verdant and 1,02 ^ 20 ≈ 1,49 for Stormfang, against a fixed ceiling of ×1,20 for twenty City Guards. The Guards are cheap and immediate, but they are the smaller lever; what makes them attractive is that they stack multiplicatively on top of the tower, not that they outweigh it.

CombatPopulation morale

In short

Attacking someone much smaller than you is penalised: the opposing defence is raised, by up to half again. Attacking someone bigger brings no bonus — morale only works in one direction.

Technical detail 
morale = max( 1 ; min( 1,5 ; ( attacker population / MAIN defender population ) ^ 0,2 ) )

Both the floor at 1 and the ceiling at 1,5 are measured: morale multiplies the total defence, flat term included, and never drops below 1,0. Seen from the other side, this amounts to dividing the attacker's effective strength, with a floor at 0,667.

The field is called "Main defender: total population": the calculation uses the population of the owner of the attacked village alone. Reinforcements, whatever their size, do not change it. A small player reinforced by a large one therefore keeps the protection of his own small population.

Exceptions: Wilder Sites have no population, hence no morale; and morale does not apply to Wonders and Ancient Fortresses held by a player.

CombatScouts

In short

On a scouting mission, scouts are opposed only by enemy scouts. Sent along with an army they are ordinary units: they count in the headcount, add nothing to the attack, and report nothing. Send them alone.

Technical detail

The scouting mission has its own law: only scouts count on both sides, with attack 35 and defence 20 — values specific to this resolution, and not the same as the printed stats the unit uses in regular combat. The defender never loses scouts, whatever the strength ratio. The attacker loses min(100 % ; (D/A)^M).

The defender only receives a scouting report if at least one attacking scout was killed: reconnaissance against a village with no scouts goes unnoticed.

CombatSiege

In short

Two weapons, two targets. Rams destroy the Watch Tower. Catapults demolish buildings and fields, up to four targets per attack. Both act only in an attack, never in a raid.

Catapults

Technical detail — demolition law 
P       = 4 × ( unit's effective attack / base attack ) × surviving headcount
cost(n) = n     points to flatten level n — whatever the building

while P > cost(current level):  P −= cost(level) ; level −= 1
strictly-greater comparison: on a tie, nothing is destroyed

Flattening a building of level L therefore costs L(L+1)/2 points, that is L(L+1)/8 catapults without upgrades. Across the ordinary buildings that were tested, the cost depended on the level alone and not on which building was targeted.

Watch out — the scope of this law

Both the coefficient 4 and the level-only cost are a model fitted to measurements on ordinary buildings, not a server constant read from the code. Extending them to the Ancient Monument, or to any endgame structure, is an extrapolation: nothing tested here rules out a different scale or a specific protection on those targets unconfirmed. Do not plan a siege on a Monument from an arithmetic projection.

Damage follows Smithy upgrades by the same ratio as attack.

Multiple targets: two targets are allowed from the start, the third requires 100 catapults and the fourth 200. The points are split evenly and fractionally between the targets, with no carry-over of the remainder. Aiming at several buildings therefore divides the power with no compensation.

Targeting can be disrupted: the Stormbrew Works and the Shadow Veil can make targets random.

Durability and rebuilding

The Stonemason's Lodge increases the durability of every building in the village by 10 % per level, strictly linearly: 1 + 0,1 × level, so ×3,0 at level 20. A destroyed building leaves recoverable rubble, and rebuilding it up to its previous level benefits from −25 % time by default.

Up to 40 % of fallen soldiers become healable — siege units, Leaders and Settlers excluded. Verdant traps

The Thornsnare Grove captures attacking units. Captured troops keep consuming the food of their home village. The number of traps follows the world speed, and capturing enemies permanently improves the speed of reinforcements leaving that village.

Reinforcements

  • The diplomatic relation decides who can reinforce whom.
  • The defences of several armies simply add up: splitting them changes nothing.
  • Each army carries its own alliance bonus, applied to its own troops only. A reinforcement from a better-equipped alliance does not improve the host village's troops.
  • In a famine, foreign reinforcements die before your own.
  • Leaving an alliance automatically sends back or recalls the reinforcements involved.

Friendly attacks

The Rally Point offers the option to allow friendly attacks on a village: alliance and pact members can then attack it, but raids and scouts stay blocked and no resources can be plundered. This is the training mechanism between allies.

Technical detail — distance and speed 
distance = √( (x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)² )

The metric is Euclidean, and this is measured, not assumed: the tile panel prints the distance to one decimal, and three readings at 5,8 · 8,6 · 10,8 match √34 · √74 · √117 exactly, where a chessboard metric would have given 5 · 7 · 9. In short

Technical detail — how to read this table

The attack ÷ upkeep ratio is the right yardstick for comparing two offensive units, because food is the real long-term constraint. For Embermark, the Iron Spear reaches 70 per point of upkeep against 45 for the Crimson Lancer: cavalry is bought for its speed and its capacity, not for its yield.

Siege units have the highest upkeep in the game (3 to 6) for a modest attack: their value is structural, not martial.

Training times are base values: the world speed and the building level reduce them, the level by 0,9 ^ (level − 1) — a Workshop displays exactly 100 % at level 1 and 90 % at level 2.

Where each unit is trained, read off the training screens of one faction: Infantry in the Barracks, Cavalry in the Stable — scouts included, which is exactly why the class matters — and Leaders and Settlers in the Residence or Palace. Eight units were listed this way. Siege is the one line not read but inferred: the Workshop describes itself as building siege machines and a catapult lists "Workshop level 10" among its requirements, but with no siege unit unlocked the Workshop's training list was empty siege placement inferred.

The Academy trains nothing: it unlocks unit types through a research paid in resources, not in Research Points. The mapping follows the Class column observed on one faction only.

Ram damage lawThe target is the Watch Tower and durability varies by faction (1× / 2× / 5×), but the calculation is not exposed by the simulator.
How the Stonemason's Lodge enters the siege formulaIts own curve is settled — durability = 1 + 0,1 × level. What is not settled is where that factor applies in the demolition law: it does not reconcile with every observed step.
Exact prerequisite for a KeystoneThe wording is "Unlock any 3 Major ranks in this branch". A guild with its three Majors at one rank each satisfies both readings at once, so the observed state does not discriminate. A guild holding three ranks in fewer than three Majors would settle it.
Visibility threshold for incoming troopsIt depends on the Rally Point level; the value is not published.
Curve of the Culture Point thresholdsConfigured by the server; only the Residence or the Palace of the current world shows the next threshold.
Per-level scale of the alliance bonusesObserved totals are world-specific. Embassy 6 plus a Banner's Reach contribution of 2 fits the observed level-2 cap of 8; a constant increase of one at every level is not established.
Configured maximum alliance capacityIt determines the Monument's cost multiplier, and varies by world.
Scaling of The Ancients' troopsAnnounced as indexed on the maximum alliance size; the formula is not demonstrated.
Complete table of Wilder Site typesThe +25 / +50 / +100% tiers are reported; the percentage displayed in game is authoritative.
Training building, beyond the eight units listedEight units were read off the training screens of one faction. Siege placement is inferred from the Workshop's own description, and the thirty units of the other three factions follow the mapping by symmetry, not by observation.
Minor→Major pairings, 23 of 27Four named links are confirmed by displayed prerequisites. Player captures also show one rank locked versus two ranks unlocked on an unnamed pair; they do not establish a fifth named link or prove every branch.
Capital + city combinationThe code reportedly gives priority to the capital branch for the fields; the combination is not documented.
Fate of over-cap fields after a capital moveA community reply reports that fields are reduced. The exact resulting level and timing have not been supplied. Do not assume a level-10 result, particularly without distinguishing village and City status.
True field ceiling in a capitalLevel 11 has been seen offered on a level-10 field, so the cap is above 10. The figure of 22 comes from the client's code and no field has been observed anywhere near it.
Wave Builder base capacityPut at 8 by veteran players independently agreeing, which makes Wave Drill a +1 per rank research. Solid enough to plan on; still testimony rather than a reading, because the game never displays the figure.
Price of a second research-capacity purchaseThe first costs 11 000 RP for Minor and 54 000 for Major. A community reply reports increasing costs; the second amount and progression remain unverified.
Research-reset cooldown scope80% refund and a 72-hour cooldown are displayed. The exact scope of the timer and whether individual/full resets share it remain unknown.
Effect carried by each blessingThe three tiers of all seven are known. Which effect text belongs to which blessing was matched by meaning, not demonstrated.
Watchfire mechanics after the September patchThe scoring tiers, supply cap and end-of-event refund in circulation predate the patch and have not been re-observed during a live event.
Celebration amount by Festival Grounds levelThe duration law is documented in the extracted table. Community feedback says only duration changes with level, not the award; this remains attributed testimony, not a multi-level reward experiment.
Scope of the catapult demolition lawThe coefficient-4 model is supported by ordinary-building tests, but recorded rounding discrepancies remain to resolve. Nothing tested extends it to the Ancient Monument or other endgame structures.
Fate of the four buildings absent from the target selectorThey cannot be chosen in the inspected selector. Community replies suggest random targeting for some and a possible last-building rule for Stonemason; the wording and conditions still need clarification. Rams target the Watch Tower separately.
Refund when cancelling an ordinary upgradeThe 80 % figure is documented for a Grand Work being funded. Whether a queued ordinary upgrade refunds anything, and at what rate, was not measured.
Building caps in a capital that is not a CityThe 22 cap follows City status. Whether a capital ever reaches 22 on those six buildings without being a City is not documented.
